Monday's scheduled YouTube sweep surfaced several OpenClaw videos that were not in the previous tracking state. None outranked today's official release and P1 cloud-runtime fix, but they are useful signs of where the public OpenClaw conversation is moving: beginner onboarding, competitive comparisons, and the increasingly common "should this replace ChatGPT?" framing.

Here are the three fresh videos worth tracking.

## Beginner Automation Gets the Headline

The most straightforward onboarding item is [OpenClaw Tutorial for Beginners | Automating Email + Calendar forever](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=161yAjOIHAw) from CodeWithHarry.

The title points at a recurring adoption theme: OpenClaw is being explained less as an abstract agent runtime and more as a practical automation layer for everyday workflows. Email and calendar automation are familiar enough for new users to understand quickly, but sensitive enough that reliability, permissions, and auditability matter.

That is good tutorial territory. It gives creators a way to show OpenClaw doing real work without requiring viewers to already understand Gateway internals, sessions, plugins, or channel routing.

## The Hermes Comparison Continues

Another fresh result, [OpenClaw vs Hermes: Is the OpenClaw update enough to beat Hermes?](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=UvKeDq0nyOg) from Hostinger Academy, shows how OpenClaw is being discussed in the broader agent-tools market.

The useful signal here is not the winner-take-all framing. It is that OpenClaw updates are now being evaluated as competitive events. That suggests creators are watching release cadence, user-facing features, and workflow coverage closely enough to turn version changes into comparison content.

For the project, those comparisons can be valuable even when they are simplified. They surface the questions new users are asking before they install:

- What can OpenClaw do that a hosted assistant cannot?
- How much setup does it require?
- Which workflows are easy today?
- Where does it still feel like infrastructure?

## The ChatGPT Replacement Question

The third new video, [Should OpenClaw Replace Your ChatGPT?](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=wfKjGwL8rdc) from Naaackers, lands on a question the OpenClaw community keeps circling.

The strongest answer is probably not a simple yes or no. OpenClaw is most compelling when users need agents connected to local tools, channels, automations, files, and long-running workflows. ChatGPT is still simpler for ordinary conversational use.

That distinction is becoming part of the public narrative. OpenClaw is not just "another chat app"; it is increasingly framed as an operating layer for agent work, especially when the work spans real systems.

## Why This Roundup Matters

YouTube is a noisy source, and many results are too broad or speculative for standalone coverage. Today's batch is still useful because it clusters around adoption: beginner tutorials, market comparisons, and replacement questions.

That is what a maturing ecosystem looks like from the outside. The codebase is shipping deep runtime fixes, while creators are translating the product into beginner workflows and competitive narratives. Both matter if OpenClaw is going to keep growing beyond early adopters.
